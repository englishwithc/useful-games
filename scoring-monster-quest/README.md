# 🏰 Monster Quest: A Gamified Classroom Companion

## 🎯 What is this?
**Monster Quest** is an immersive, single-page RPG web application designed to gamify classroom tasks using **Variable Ratio Reinforcement**. 

Instead of a boring checklist, students are "Heroes" on a quest. They choose a companion (Starter Monster), fight enemies, solve puzzles, and earn upgrades to progress through the dungeon. The goal is to transform repetitive lessons into an active adventure where every small task brings them closer to defeating the "Boss."

---

## 🧠 The Strategy: Why This Works
This tool is built on a psychological principle called **Variable Ratio Reinforcement**. 

| Traditional Approach | Monster Quest Approach |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fixed Reward:** "If you finish this list, you get X points." (Boring/Fixed) | **Variable Reward:** You never know *when* the next reward will come. High anticipation! |
| **Passive:** Students wait for a teacher to grade them. | **Active:** Students make choices, take risks ("Charge"), and see immediate visual feedback. |
| **Disconnected:** The game is separate from the lesson. | **Integrated:** The "Quest" is the motivation; the "Task" is the action. |

### 🧩 How It Motivates
1.  **Dopamine Loop:** The unpredictable nature of rewards keeps students in "hunt mode."
2.  **Low Barrier to Entry:** Students can choose a path (Battle, Puzzle, or Reward) instantly using visual cues and emojis.
3.  **Agency:** They aren't just completing work; they are making *strategic choices* about their hero's stats (damage vs. defense).

## 👩‍🏫 How to Use It in the Classroom

### The Setup
1.  **Hosted on Github pages. Access the site link to play the game.

### The Loop: Task → Reward → Work
Do not pause your lesson to let them play the whole game. Use the game as a **motivation trigger**.

1.  **The Hook:** At the start of the lesson, ask students to choose their "Starter Monster." This gives them ownership immediately.
2.  **Call Out a Task:** As you call out a classroom task (e.g., *"Write three sentences about your weekend"* or *"Solve these equations"*):
    > *"Finish that quickly! Then come to the screen and choose a path!"*
3.  **The Action:** The student completes the task on their paper/whiteboard, then clicks the button on the Monster Quest screen.
4.  **The Payoff:** They click "Attack" or "Puzzle" in the game. If they win, they earn an upgrade (visual HP increase) and move to the next floor.

### 🛠️ Implementation Tips for Teachers
*   **No Installation Needed:** It runs in any modern web browser. No apps required.
*   **Visual Learning:** The game uses visual cues (colors, health bars) rather than complex text instructions, making it accessible for ESL students or those with dyslexia.
*   **Flexible Pathing:** Students can choose:
    *   ⚔️ **Battle:** Fight an enemy to gain XP (Risk/Reward).
    *   🧩 **Puzzle:** Solve a color/number pattern (Low cognitive load, visual focus).
    *   🎁 **Reward:** Complete a specific task to upgrade their monster.

## 🎮 Game Mechanics for Teachers

### 1. The Starter Monsters
At the start of class, students pick an avatar:
*   **Ember:** High damage (Good for aggressive learners).
*   **Aqua:** Balanced healing (Good for steady progress).
*   **Terra:** High defense (Good for risk-averse learners).

### 2. The Three Paths
When the student is ready to engage, they are presented with three paths on the map:
1.  **Battle:** Fight an enemy. They must use their equipped abilities (Quick Hit, Guard, etc.). This introduces simple math and resource management.
2.  **Puzzle:** Solve a "Magic Seal." This is great for visual learners or students who need a break from writing. It uses color memory and number counting.
3.  **Reward:** They can choose between a **Safe Chest** (guaranteed small reward) or a **Gamble Chest** (50% chance of big rewards, 50% chance of taking damage). This teaches risk management!

### 3. Progression
The game is designed to be short-ish and snappy. A full run through the dungeon takes quite a bit of time so multiple actions after one task are advisable. Visual progress is tracked by HP bars and floor numbers, providing instant feedback on effort.
